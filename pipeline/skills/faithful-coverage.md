# faithful-coverage

The page should let the reader skip re-reading the section, so it must carry everything the section
carries, and nothing it doesn't.

**Cover, in the book's order and numbering:** every concept and definition; every field, flag, default
and limit; every listing (as `:::code`, highlighting the lines the book annotates); every command and
its output (as `:::term`, or a `:::snippet` for output the shell can't produce); every table (same rows);
every figure (as your own diagram of the same idea, never a redraw of the book's layout); every
note, tip, warning and sidebar (as a callout); "at the time of writing" caveats and version notes.

**Write it in your own words.** Explain the idea the way you would at a whiteboard: shorter sentences,
restructured, your own examples where they help. Do not copy or lightly reword the book's sentences.
Technical terms, field names, commands and standard tool output formats are fine verbatim.

**Add, don't drift.** You may add a one-line clarification, a "why it matters", a comparison with an
earlier chapter, or a gotcha. Never add facts that aren't in the source section (no version numbers,
dates, vendor comparisons or product news from your own knowledge); the judge flags unsupported claims.

Checklist before you finish:
- every listing, command, table and figure from the brief's inventory appears once
- numbers, names and defaults match the source exactly
- nothing important is only in a diagram; the prose says it too
- prose budget from the brief respected (visuals carry the rest)
